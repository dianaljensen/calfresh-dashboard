Error rates

Rates are cumulative for the federal fiscal year. 19 PMC counties report their own rates; the other 39 share a pooled rate. Rates lag the participation file. Not a county ranking.

What these rates mean

Active error rate looks at households already getting CalFresh. It asks: did they get the right amount? “Active” means the case was on the program — not that an error is happening right now.

Negative error rate looks at denials and closings. It asks: was that “no” correct? “Negative” means the county said no — not that the number is worse.

Neither is a fraud rate. The two answer different questions, so the percents are not comparable. The negative rate is often much higher.

Sources Data: CDSS CalFresh Data Dashboard Monthly tab (Active Error Rate, Negative Error Rate, Negative Error Rate on Completed Cases). Definitions CalFresh is the name for the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) in California. Quality Control reviews a sample of county eligibility and benefit decisions. An “error” is the reviewer finding that the decision or the amount was wrong — not fraud. Active means the household was already getting CalFresh (wrong amount or should not have been certified). Negative means the county said no — a denial or a closing (that “no” was wrong, or a required step was skipped). Data notes
  • Rates are cumulative for the federal fiscal year, not a single-month snapshot.
  • 19 Project Management County (PMC) counties report their own rates. The other 39 counties share one pooled rate, so several county lines can sit on top of each other.
  • QC rates lag caseload reporting. Months with no published rate are shown as gaps. This is not a county ranking bar.
Wonky & math-y details

Quality Control

  • Active reviews are of cases receiving benefits (USDA’s payment-accuracy universe). Negative reviews are of denials, terminations, and suspensions (USDA now calls that measure CAPER; CDSS still publishes it as Negative Error Rate).
  • Hover on Negative Error Rate includes Negative Error Rate on Completed Cases (n).
  • Selecting two counties does not average their rates. Each selected place is its own overlay line.
