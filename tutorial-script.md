## 0:00 — What this is

This is the CalFresh Data Explorer. I created it to take public CalFresh data — participation, applications, churn, and quality control — and puts them in one place where people explore trends over time, by county, region, and/or county size. It layers many of the more nuanced data that are available about subpopulations into new data visualizations.

This is a working prototype. The numbers come from the CDSS CalFresh Data Dashboard, the CF296 Caseload Movement Report and the CF18 Churn Report. If something looks wrong, or if a different view would help you do your job, let me know!. My email address is on the page: diana@djconsults.com.

## 0:35 — Who it’s for

I used to be a planning analyst at a county, and I later worked as a CalFresh advocate. I’m a consultant now working with many different types of stakeholders. County staff and advocates are often looking at the same caseload from different perspectives. This tool is meant to be usable from either one: a way to see what is happening statewide, and then the same question for individual counties.

## 1:05 — Four pages, four questions

There are four pages in the Data Explorer.

Participation Trends asks who is on the program, and whether that number is rising or falling.

Applications asks what happened to people who applied: approved, withdrawn, denied as ineligible, or denied for a procedural reason. It also shows how long decisions took.

Churn asks what happened to households that already had CalFresh and had a recertification or a Semi-Annual Report, SAR7, due.

Quality Control looks at federally reported error rates: in the federal review sample, were benefit amounts right, and were denials and closings right. These are not a fraud rate.

## 2:05 — Filters, on Participation

I’ll stay on Participation. The page opens on California, for the last 24 months.

[Hover a month on Total CalFresh Participants.] The box above the chart is the value for that month.

[Expand the filter bar.] 
You can change the date range using the slider, or the presets. 

Geography is three lists: Counties, County Size, and Regions. In this section, Regions and county size replace each other. California stays in the county list.

[Select one size group, for example Large. Then Drill into group.]
If exactly one region or one size group is selected, Drill into group lists the counties in that group and keeps the group on the chart.

Clear the filters using this Clear filters button.

[Clear back to only one county. Turn on Add statewide comparison.] The dashed line is California. It is a reference, not a target. These charts have a toggle to easily add a statewide comparison.

## 3:35 — Change, then the flow of cases

[Scroll to Annual % Change.] 
We have two views of % change: The first shows % change in participation numbers compared to the same month in the prior year. You can see compared to the three months prior, if you prefer.

You can also see the same data for a given month snapshot, below. On this view, you can group by size or region. The dashed line is the combined rate for whatever grouping you've chosen. If you'd prefer to see fewer counties, you can use the filters to get a set that is useful for you. 

[Scroll to Caseload movement.] 
The final chart on this page is Caseload Movement. This is the flow in and out in a particular month: approvals and other additions above the line, discontinuances below, and net as additions minus discontinuances. A flat caseload can still be a lot of people coming on and a lot of people leaving. This is where you see that.

## 4:35 — Applications

[Open Applications. Leave it on California.] 
Let's look at Applications. The stacked charts show the mix of outcomes for applications with a final disposition in each month: approved, withdrawn, denied as ineligible, and procedurally denied.

It is worth noting that a procedural denial means the case ended for a process reason, not because the county found the household explicitly ineligible. The chart doesn't include the reason for the denial. Read it as a place to ask a better question, in your county or in a county you partner with.

For Application Outcomes, there's a lot of data to explore. You can see these data for different places, or populations.
[Toggle the top Compare buttons]

[Scroll to What is the impact of procedural denials? Do not read the dollar figure.]

Under the chart, there are a variety Applications-related data trends you can unfold. [Show a few, not all]

You can show them as #s or %s. Not all data elements are available for each population.

This box is a what-if: one month of benefits if 1% of that month’s applications had been approved instead of procedurally denied, for the place you selected, knowing some of those households may not have been eligible.

Just like on the Participation page, you can see a one-month snapshot across all counties. You can toggle between the populations.

Further down, Timeliness is the share decided after the deadline, and average days to process. It follows the same date and place from the filters above. It is not a ranking.

## 6:05 — Churn

[Open Churn. Stay on California, latest month.] Churn is the cycle where a household does not complete a regular report and later comes back through a full application. Households that were still eligible miss benefits during the gap. Counties pick up a full application that might have been a recertification or a SAR 7.

The page shows that two ways. Recertification / SAR 7 Outcomes is: of households that had one of those reports due, what happened? No loss of benefits, late with a loss, ineligible, no return, or a later reapplication. The orange band on this page is that later application.

[Open Reapplication Outcomes] Of course not everyone who reapplies is eligible. We can see that breakdown here.

[Open Time to Reapply] And some people reapply sooner than others, which we can see here. The eligibility of those reapplicants can be found in the tooltop on this chart.

[Open Estimated Lost Benefits. Do not read the dollar figures.] Late with loss estimates the days of benefits missed by households that turned the report in late, and a back-of-the-envelope calculation for the benefits they lost if they remained eligible in between.


Applicants’ Prior CalFresh Connection asks a different question: of applications disposed that month, how many were from people recently on the program due, versus people who were not recently on the program. We can see, for those that were on, whether they had a report due. This gives a sense of how much application work is related to returning cases.

[Scroll to the county line-ups]
Both types of churn metrics are shown in a monthly view. You can choose whether to show only SAR7s, only Recerts, or Combined. You can sort by different variables.

## 7:20 — Quality control, and how not to over-read

[Open Quality Control.] Active error rate is households already getting CalFresh: did they get the right amount? Negative error rate is denials and closings: was that no correct? Neither is a fraud rate. The two percents answer different questions, so do not compare them to each other. The negative rate is often much higher. Nineteen counties have their own rate. The other thirty-nine share a pooled rate. A line is not always that county’s number.

## 8:05 — Close

I hope this set of visualizations helps to make state data explorable for a larger set of people. If you have suggestions or would like some help figuring out how to use this tool for your work, please do reach out to me.

## Recording on a Mac, editing on a PC

Record in **Zoom**. It puts your face and the dashboard into one file, and that file is an MP4 Windows can open. The Mac screenshot tool (Command–Shift–5) records the screen and your voice only, not your camera.

A raw Mac screenshot recording is a different kind of file, and Windows often cannot open it without a paid codec. You are not using that tool, so you do not need to convert anything before you send the Zoom file.

### Set Zoom once

1. Open Zoom. Click the gear, then **Recording**.
2. Turn on **Record video during screen sharing**.
3. Leave **Place video next to the shared screen in the recording** off, if you see it. Your face stays a small corner image and the dashboard stays large.
4. Turn off **Add a timestamp to the recording**, if you see it.

### While you record

Do a ten-second test first. Play it back and confirm you can see your face, the browser, and hear your voice in the same file.

1. Turn on Do Not Disturb. Open the dashboard on Participation Trends, loaded, with the filter bar collapsed.
2. In Zoom, start a **New meeting** by yourself. Start your video. In the meeting, click the arrow next to the microphone and pick your mic. Sit with light on your face.
3. Click **Share**, and share the **browser window**, not the whole desktop. That keeps the Zoom controls out of the picture.
4. Click **Record**, then **Record on this computer**.
5. Wait a short pause, then start the script.
6. If you stumble, put the screen back to where that sentence started, say **“again”**, then redo the sentence and the clicks. “Again” is the signal to cut. The picture before that word should already match the picture you want to keep.
7. Leave a short pause after the last sentence. Stop the recording, then end the meeting.
8. Wait until Zoom finishes processing. It will open a folder in Documents, under Zoom.

Send the file whose name contains `shared_screen_with_speaker_view` and ends in `.mp4`, plus this script. Ignore any extra audio-only file in that folder. Keep the original until you have watched the trimmed cut.

If Clipchamp will not import that MP4, convert it with the free app [HandBrake](https://handbrake.fr/). Open the Zoom file, choose preset **Fast 1080p30**, set **Format** to **MP4**, and save `calfresh-data-explorer-tutorial.mp4`.

### Trimming on the PC

Use **Clipchamp**, which is already installed on Windows 11. Open it from the Start menu. If it is not there, install the free **CapCut** desktop app and use the same cuts. This pass is only trimming. Do not add music, titles, captions, zooms, arrows, or color changes. Do not crop the screen, speed the video up, or reorder sections.

1. In Clipchamp, create a new video and choose **Import media**. Select the Zoom file whose name contains `shared_screen_with_speaker_view`.
2. Drag the clip onto the timeline.
3. Play until just before a mistake, the word “again,” or a long silence. Click **Split** (or press **S**).
4. Play to the first frame of the good take. **Split** again.
5. Click the bad piece in the middle and press **Delete**. The two good sides should meet.
6. Play across that join with the sound on. The sentence should not repeat a word, and the screen should not flash a half-finished click. If it does, undo and move the split.
7. Also trim the blank time at the very beginning and after the last word. Leave about half a second of quiet at each end so the first and last words are not chopped.
8. Watch the whole video once before exporting.
9. Choose **Export**, then **1080p**. Save `calfresh-data-explorer-tutorial-trimmed.mp4`.
